WHERE AUSTRALIA PLAYS

Who moves, what we play, and the invisible line that splits the country's football.

Emilio Pinelo Landarte 37497731 · October 2026

Australia likes to think of itself as a sporting nation. The national AusPlay survey, which asks tens of thousands of Australians every year what they do to stay active, shows a more interesting picture: almost everyone moves, far fewer play organised sport, and which sport you play still depends on which side of an old, invisible line you grew up on.

1We all move. Fewer of us play.

Picture 100 Australian adults. In 2025, 86 of them did some physical activity at least once, from a walk around the block to a weekly netball game. Only 14 did nothing at all.

But only 42 took part in something organised: a club, a class, a team, or a paid program. The other 44 kept moving on their own terms, mostly walking, running or going to the gym alone.

AusPlay counts an activity as "organised" when a club, association, gym, school or other organisation runs it.

4Where we play: the Barassi Line

3,023 football grounds draw the Barassi Line

Every Australian football and rugby league ground mapped in OpenStreetMap. One dot = one ground. Click the legend to isolate a code.

In his 1978 Ron Barassi Memorial Lecture, the historian Ian Turner named an imaginary line that runs down Queensland's western border, then south-east through western New South Wales to Cape Howe on the Victorian border. It became known as the Barassi Line.

West and south of the line, Australians play Australian football. North and east of it, they play rugby league.

Nearly 50 years later the grounds still follow the line: Victoria has 1,271 AFL grounds and just 11 rugby league grounds, while New South Wales has more rugby league grounds than AFL ones.

OpenStreetMap is mapped by volunteers and covers cities better than the bush, so this map shows grounds that are mapped, not a full census.

The survey tells the same story with people instead of grounds, and shows which states are the most active overall.

TAKEAWAYS

  • Almost every adult moves, but fewer than half play organised sport, and the share keeps falling with age.
  • Individual fitness (gym, Pilates, bushwalking) is growing fastest.
  • Football is still regional: the Barassi Line shows up in both the grounds and the players.

ABOUT THE DATA

Participation figures are survey estimates for adults (15+) from AusPlay, January–December 2025, unless stated. Rankings compare 2017–2022 (phone survey) with 2024–2025 (online survey) separately. Estimates with a relative margin of error above 100% are not shown.
